How it works

Plain English
  1. Like for like only. Drivers are compared inside one car, on one track layout, in one iRacing season, at one series weight. A balance or tyre change moves everyone in that car together, so it cancels out.
  2. Pace is the average of your best five clean, dry laps, not one lap that might be a tow.
  3. Equal practice. The more laps you turn, the quicker your best five gets. We measured this from the team's own lap histories: after 10 laps the best five is typically 1.5% slower than after 200, after 30 laps 0.8%, after 100 laps 0.2%. Every driver is rated as if they had done the same number of laps.
  4. Track temperature. Each degree hotter costs about 0.06% of lap time, so a cool-track lap is not credited as talent.
  1. One rating per driver that best explains every comparison at once. It uses the size of the gap, not just who won, and it accounts for who you happened to share the car and track with.
  2. Thin data is treated with caution. A driver with few laps is pulled towards the team average and given a wide range. One-off wild results count for less (212 of 1257 were down-weighted).
  3. Road racing only, both Garage 61 teams (Pro and Casual), all four 2026 seasons (16 December 2025 to 16 November 2026).
  4. Car type barely matters. The model lets each driver be better in one class than another. The effect is small (about 0.13%, against 0.67% between drivers): quick drivers are quick in everything.

Does seat time make you faster?

Hours on track against pace

In one car at one track: a lot, quickly

How far a driver is from the pace they eventually reach there, by how much clean running they have done. After 10 minutes of clean laps the typical gap is 2.1%, after an hour 0.6%, and it is all but gone by four hours.

Across the team: a little

One dot per ranked driver. Drivers with more hours in 2026 tend to be quicker, about 0.24% per doubling of hours, but hours explain only about 14% of the difference between drivers.

Over the year: barely. Each doubling of a driver's own total hours made them about 0.04% quicker against the rest of the team, and drivers who put in more hours in 2026 did not improve more than those who put in fewer. Seat time pays off in the car and track you are about to race, which is why the ranking corrects for practice inside each comparison and not for total hours.

Hours are Garage 61 time on track from 16 December 2025 to 16 November 2026, with time sitting idle in a session removed; hours for two drivers are estimated from their laps. The first chart uses 351 driver and car/track pairs with at least two hours of clean laps, and clean laps are roughly 40% of total seat time. Garage 61 only counts hours since each driver installed it, so the link across the team is not proof that hours cause pace.

What it cannot see

Read with these in mind
  • Only laps recorded by Garage 61. Nothing from before a driver installed it, and nothing from drivers who do not run it.
  • Setups, fuel load, slipstream and tyre age are not controlled. Best-five and the range absorb some of this, not all.
  • This is one-lap and short-run pace. Racecraft, traffic and mistakes are not in it. Stint spread is shown beside it for that reason.
  • True pace assumes equal practice. In a real event the driver who prepares more will be quicker than their rating suggests: the As driven column shows that.
